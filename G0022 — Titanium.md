Main G0022 result

Titanium gives Genesis several particularly important results.

The first is the transition-metal electronic sequence:

\boxed{
Sc:d^1
\rightarrow
Ti:d^2
}

but titanium can subsequently become:

Ti^{3+}:d^1

or:

Ti^{4+}:d^0.

So the relevant d-electron count belongs to the state, not merely the parent element.

\boxed{
TiO_2
-

\rightarrow

\rightarrow
\text{conductivity change}
}

and sufficiently organised reduction produces conductive Magnéli structures such as Ti₄O₇. 

That means a custom material can potentially be designed not only by asking:

\text{“Which atoms do I add?”}

but also:

\boxed{
\text{“Which atoms do I deliberately remove, where do I remove them, and how do I arrange the vacancies?”}
}

Third, titanium provides a direct material-memory pathway:

\boxed{
E_{\rm applied}
\rightarrow
\text{oxygen-vacancy rearrangement}
\rightarrow
R_{\rm changed}
}

so atomic defects can encode device state.

\boxed{
TiO_2
+
C
+
Cl_2
\rightarrow
TiCl_4
\xrightarrow[\text{Ar}]{Mg}
Ti
}

so our future materials engine cannot optimise the final material alone; it must also optimise its path of manufacture.

Ti_{\rm elemental}
\xrightarrow{P\sim248\,GPa}
T_c\sim26K

TiN
