# MAT Materials Science Framework

## Purpose

Materials science connects composition, structure, processing and properties.

A central MAT relationship is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Processing}
\rightarrow
\text{Structure}
\rightarrow
\text{Properties}
\rightarrow
\text{Performance}
}
\]

Composition and environment interact with every stage.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-001 — Processing-Structure-Property Relationship

Material properties depend not only on composition but also on how the material was produced.

Conceptually:

\[
P=
F(C,S,H,E)
\]

where:

- \(C\) = composition;
- \(S\) = structure;
- \(H\) = processing history;
- \(E\) = environment.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-002 — Crystal Structure

A crystalline material is characterized by long-range periodic order.

Relevant fields include:

- crystal system;
- space group;
- lattice vectors;
- basis;
- atomic coordinates.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-003 — Bravais Lattice

Three-dimensional periodic crystals can be classified using the 14 Bravais lattice types.

This classification should not be confused with space-group classification.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-004 — Unit Cell

A crystal structure may be generated by translation of a unit cell.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-005 — Miller Indices

Crystal planes may be represented:

\[
(hkl)
\]

Directions may be represented:

\[
[uvw]
\]

Conventions depend on crystal system.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-006 — Bragg Diffraction

\[
n\lambda=2d\sin\theta
\]

under the idealized Bragg condition.

Used in diffraction-based structural characterization.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-007 — Reciprocal Space

Diffraction and band-structure descriptions are often naturally expressed in reciprocal space.

MAT may maintain both real-space and reciprocal-space representations.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-008 — Crystal Defects

Defect classes include:

- vacancies;
- interstitials;
- substitutional defects;
- dislocations;
- grain boundaries;
- stacking faults;
- interfaces.

Defects may dominate properties even at low concentration.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-009 — Grain Structure

Polycrystalline properties depend on:

- grain size;
- shape;
- orientation;
- boundary character.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-010 — Hall-Petch-Type Relation

For many materials and grain-size regimes:

\[
\sigma_y
=
\sigma_0
+
k_yd^{-1/2}
\]

This is empirical and is not universal at arbitrarily small grain sizes.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-011 — Stress

\[
\sigma=\frac{F}{A}
\]

for simple normal engineering stress.

More general states require a stress tensor.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-012 — Strain

Engineering strain:

\[
\epsilon=
\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}
\]

Other strain measures are required for large deformation.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-013 — Elastic Modulus

In uniaxial linear elasticity:

\[
E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}
\]

within the elastic regime.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-014 — Poisson Ratio

\[
\nu
=
-\frac{\epsilon_\text{transverse}}
{\epsilon_\text{axial}}
\]

under the applicable deformation regime.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-015 — Yield

Yield indicates transition from predominantly elastic response into irreversible deformation under the specified test definition.

Yield criterion depends on material and stress state.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-016 — Plasticity

Plastic deformation may involve:

- dislocation glide;
- twinning;
- diffusion;
- phase transformation;
- grain-boundary mechanisms.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-017 — Fracture Toughness

Fracture resistance depends on:

- crack geometry;
- loading mode;
- specimen conditions;
- microstructure.

A toughness value requires test context.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-018 — Fatigue

Failure may occur after repeated cyclic loading below monotonic strength limits.

State requires:

- stress range;
- mean stress;
- frequency;
- cycle count;
- environment.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-019 — Creep

Time-dependent deformation occurs under sustained stress, especially at elevated homologous temperature.

Thus:

\[
\epsilon=
F(\sigma,T,t,\text{microstructure})
\]

---

# MAT-MATSCI-020 — Hardness

Hardness depends on test method.

Possible scales include:

- Vickers;
- Rockwell;
- Brinell;
- nanoindentation hardness.

Never compare scales without appropriate conversion/qualification.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-021 — Toughness

Toughness is not identical to hardness or strength.

MAT keeps these properties separate.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-022 — Porosity

\[
\phi=
\frac{V_\text{void}}
{V_\text{total}}
\]

Porosity topology and connectivity may be as important as total fraction.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-023 — Composite Rule of Mixtures

Some composite properties may be approximated through constituent-weighted relationships.

Applicability depends strongly on geometry, interfaces and loading.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-024 — Phase Fraction

Multi-phase materials require explicit phase fractions where known.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-025 — Phase Diagram

Phase stability depends on variables such as:

- temperature;
- pressure;
- composition.

A phase diagram therefore represents a slice of MAT state space.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-026 — Microstructure Evolution

Processes such as:

- recrystallization;
- precipitation;
- grain growth;
- phase transformation;

alter properties over time.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-027 — Diffusion in Solids

Diffusion may depend on:

- temperature;
- defects;
- concentration;
- crystal orientation;
- phase.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-028 — Arrhenius Diffusion

A common form:

\[
D=D_0e^{-Q/(RT)}
\]

where applicable.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-029 — Thermal Expansion

Linear approximation:

\[
\Delta L
=
\alpha L_0\Delta T
\]

over a range where \(\alpha\) can reasonably be treated as constant.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-030 — Thermal Shock

Thermal gradients can generate mechanical stress.

Assessment may require:

- thermal expansion;
- elastic modulus;
- thermal conductivity;
- fracture strength/toughness.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-031 — Corrosion

Corrosion depends on the coupled state:

\[
\text{material}
+
\text{environment}
+
\text{electrochemistry}
+
t
\]

rather than intrinsic composition alone.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-032 — Wear

Wear mechanisms include:

- adhesive;
- abrasive;
- erosive;
- fatigue;
- tribochemical.

Test geometry and loading are essential.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-033 — Friction

Friction coefficient is a property of an interface/system rather than simply one isolated material.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-034 — Anisotropy

Properties may depend on direction:

\[
P=P(\hat n)
\]

Examples:

- elastic modulus;
- conductivity;
- refractive index;
- fracture.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-035 — Texture

Preferred crystallographic orientation can make a polycrystal macroscopically anisotropic.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-036 — Surface Effects

As scale decreases:

\[
\frac{A}{V}
\]

often increases, making surfaces increasingly influential.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-037 — Nanomaterial Regime

Nanoscale properties may differ because of:

- surface effects;
- confinement;
- grain-size effects;
- finite dimensions;
- altered phonon transport.

Bulk values must not automatically be copied into nanoscale records.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-038 — Thin Film Effects

Thin films may differ from bulk due to:

- substrate strain;
- texture;
- interface chemistry;
- reduced thickness;
- deposition conditions.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-039 — Metastability

A material can persist in a local free-energy minimum even when a lower-energy state exists.

This is central to process-history dependence.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-040 — Manufacturing History

The final material should therefore retain:

```text
PROCESS HISTORY
```

rather than only final composition.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-041 — Magnetic Domain Engineering

Engineered magnetization should record:

- domain orientation;
- magnetizing field;
- pole geometry;
- magnetization direction;
- remanence.

This is distinct from intrinsic elemental magnetic response.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-042 — Electrical Microstructure Effects

Electrical behaviour can depend on:

- grain boundaries;
- defects;
- impurities;
- phase fraction;
- interfaces.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-043 — Failure Analysis

Failure may be reconstructed from:

- fracture surface;
- deformation;
- corrosion;
- thermal damage;
- microstructure;
- residual stress.

This provides a practical example of backward state inference in MAT.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-044 — Structure Hierarchy

A material may be represented at:

```text
ATOMIC
↓
NANO
↓
MICRO
↓
MESO
↓
MACRO
```

Properties can emerge differently at each level.

---

# MAT-MATSCI-045 — Performance

Application performance may depend on a vector:

\[
\mathbf P=
[
P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_n
]
\]

rather than one single property.

This supports multi-objective material design.

---
