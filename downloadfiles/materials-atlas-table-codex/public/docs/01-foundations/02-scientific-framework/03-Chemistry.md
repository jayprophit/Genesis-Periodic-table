# MAT Chemistry Framework

## Purpose

Chemistry connects atomic identity and electronic structure to bonding, reactions, compounds and materials.

---

# MAT-CHEM-001 — Conservation of Atoms in Ordinary Chemical Reactions

For ordinary chemical reactions, atomic nuclei are rearranged but are not ordinarily transformed into different elements.

Balanced reactions conserve each element count.

Example:

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O
\]

Nuclear reactions are treated separately.

---

# MAT-CHEM-002 — Conservation of Charge

A balanced chemical reaction conserves total electric charge.

---

# MAT-CHEM-003 — Stoichiometry

For:

\[
aA+bB\rightarrow cC+dD
\]

stoichiometric coefficients determine molar relationships.

---

# MAT-CHEM-004 — Amount of Substance

\[
n=\frac{m}{M}
\]

where:

- \(n\) = amount;
- \(m\) = mass;
- \(M\) = molar mass.

---

# MAT-CHEM-005 — Avogadro Relationship

\[
N=nN_A
\]

where \(N_A\) is the Avogadro constant.

---

# MAT-CHEM-006 — Ideal Gas Law

\[
PV=nRT
\]

Applicable only where the ideal-gas approximation is adequate.

---

# MAT-CHEM-007 — Partial Pressure

For an ideal gas mixture:

\[
P_\text{total}
=
\sum_iP_i
\]

---

# MAT-CHEM-008 — Chemical Equilibrium

For:

\[
aA+bB
\rightleftharpoons
cC+dD
\]

a thermodynamic equilibrium constant may be constructed from activities.

Precise treatment uses activities rather than raw concentration where non-ideal effects matter.

---

# MAT-CHEM-009 — Reaction Quotient

\[
Q
\]

has the same structural form as the equilibrium expression but uses the current state.

Comparison with \(K\) indicates direction toward equilibrium under the model.

---

# MAT-CHEM-010 — Mass Action Framework

Reaction rate/equilibrium behaviour can depend on reactant activities or concentrations.

Applicability depends on mechanism and non-ideality.

---

# MAT-CHEM-011 — Acidity

\[
pH=-\log_{10}a_{H^+}
\]

where rigorous treatment uses hydrogen-ion activity.

---

# MAT-CHEM-012 — Acid Dissociation

\[
K_a
\]

quantifies acid equilibrium under defined conditions.

---

# MAT-CHEM-013 — Redox

Oxidation and reduction involve electron-transfer accounting.

Oxidation:

```text
loss of electron density / increase in oxidation state
```

Reduction:

```text
gain of electron density / decrease in oxidation state
```

depending on the formal framework.

---

# MAT-CHEM-014 — Electrochemical Potential

Electrochemical behaviour depends on both chemical and electrical contributions.

---

# MAT-CHEM-015 — Nernst Equation

For an electrochemical reaction under appropriate assumptions:

\[
E=
E^\circ-
\frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q
\]

---

# MAT-CHEM-016 — Gibbs Reaction Criterion

At constant temperature and pressure, reaction direction is related to Gibbs free-energy change.

\[
\Delta G
=
\Delta G^\circ
+
RT\ln Q
\]

---

# MAT-CHEM-017 — Arrhenius Equation

\[
k=Ae^{-E_a/(RT)}
\]

A common model for temperature-dependent reaction rates.

Not universal for all kinetics.

---

# MAT-CHEM-018 — Reaction Rate

Generic:

\[
r=
-\frac{1}{\nu_i}
\frac{d[i]}{dt}
\]

with appropriate stoichiometric normalization.

---

# MAT-CHEM-019 — Rate Law

Example:

\[
r=k[A]^m[B]^n
\]

Reaction orders must be determined or justified; they need not equal stoichiometric coefficients.

---

# MAT-CHEM-020 — Activation Energy

A kinetic barrier affects reaction rate.

Thermodynamic favourability alone does not determine reaction speed.

---

# MAT-CHEM-021 — Catalysis

A catalyst changes the accessible reaction pathway and kinetics without changing the equilibrium thermodynamic state for the same external conditions.

---

# MAT-CHEM-022 — Bond Energy

Bond dissociation energy is context-specific and may depend on molecular environment.

MAT must avoid treating one generic bond-energy number as universally exact.

---

# MAT-CHEM-023 — Bond Order

Bond order may be represented differently under:

- Lewis theory;
- molecular orbital theory;
- bond-order metrics from quantum chemistry.

The definition should be identified.

---

# MAT-CHEM-024 — Electronegativity

Electronegativity is model-dependent.

Scales such as:

- Pauling;
- Mulliken;
- Allen;

should remain separately identified.

---

# MAT-CHEM-025 — Oxidation State

Oxidation state is a formal electron-accounting construct and should not automatically be equated with measured atomic charge.

---

# MAT-CHEM-026 — Molecular Geometry

Geometry may be modelled through:

- electron-domain approaches;
- quantum calculations;
- crystallography;
- spectroscopy.

---

# MAT-CHEM-027 — Intermolecular Forces

May include:

- electrostatic;
- hydrogen bonding;
- dispersion;
- dipole interactions;
- induction.

---

# MAT-CHEM-028 — Solubility

Solubility depends on:

- solvent;
- temperature;
- pressure;
- phase;
- composition.

It is not a single material constant independent of environment.

---

# MAT-CHEM-029 — Partitioning

Species may distribute between phases according to equilibrium/kinetic relationships.

---

# MAT-CHEM-030 — Adsorption

Surface adsorption models may include:

- Langmuir;
- Freundlich;
- BET;
- other domain-specific models.

Each has assumptions.

---

# MAT-CHEM-031 — Chemical Potential

\[
\mu_i
=
\left(
\frac{\partial G}
{\partial n_i}
\right)_{T,P,n_{j\ne i}}
\]

Chemical potential is fundamental to equilibrium and phase behaviour.

---

# MAT-CHEM-032 — Activity

For nonideal systems, activity replaces concentration as the appropriate thermodynamic quantity.

---

# MAT-CHEM-033 — Phase Equilibrium

At equilibrium:

\[
\mu_i^{(\alpha)}
=
\mu_i^{(\beta)}
\]

for a component \(i\) across coexisting phases.

---

# MAT-CHEM-034 — Le Châtelier-Type Response

Equilibrium systems respond to changed constraints.

Precise prediction should be based on thermodynamics rather than treating the qualitative principle as an equation.

---

# MAT-CHEM-035 — Chemical Kinetics Network

Complex chemistry may be represented as a reaction graph:

\[
R_1\rightarrow R_2\rightarrow\dots
\]

with branching, reversible reactions and intermediates.

---

# MAT-CHEM-036 — Isotope Effects

Isotopic substitution can alter:

- vibrational frequencies;
- reaction kinetics;
- equilibrium;
- diffusion.

Element identity alone therefore does not always completely describe chemical behaviour.

---

# MAT-CHEM-037 — Photochemistry

Photon absorption can modify electronic state and reaction pathways.

Relevant photon energy:

\[
E=h\nu
\]

---

# MAT-CHEM-038 — Electrochemistry

Reactions may be driven or measured through:

- electrode potentials;
- current;
- ion transport;
- interfacial kinetics.

---

# MAT-CHEM-039 — Surface Chemistry

Surface composition may differ substantially from bulk composition.

Surface state should therefore be separable from bulk material state.

---

# MAT-CHEM-040 — Chemical Stability

"Stable" must always be contextual.

A substance may be:

- thermodynamically stable;
- kinetically persistent;
- air-stable;
- water-stable;
- photostable;
- stable only over a particular time range.
