# Causali E Scientific Status

## Classification

Causali E is currently classified within MAT as:

**Author conceptual framework / developing hypothesis**

It is not currently classified as an independently established law of mathematics or physics.

This distinction protects both the scientific integrity of MAT and the historical integrity of the author's work.

---

## Established Concepts Related to the Framework

Parts of the conceptual territory explored by Causali E overlap with established fields including:

- causal inference;
- dynamical systems;
- state-space modelling;
- conservation laws;
- statistical mechanics;
- probability theory;
- Bayesian inference;
- control theory;
- information theory;
- nonlinear dynamics;
- graph theory;
- differential equations;
- quantum state evolution.

Similarity does not establish equivalence.

Each proposed Causali E relationship must be mapped explicitly before claiming correspondence with an established scientific theory.

## Mathematical Requirement

A mathematical statement must define:

1. its variables;
2. its domains;
3. its units where applicable;
4. its operators;
5. its constraints;
6. its initial conditions;
7. its boundary conditions;
8. its expected outputs;
9. the conditions under which it can be falsified.

Expressions using symbols such as \(+\), \(-\), \(\times\) and \(\div\) must not be assumed to represent ordinary arithmetic if they are intended to mean conceptual transformations.

In those cases a transformation operator should be defined explicitly.

For example:

\[
C = F(A,B)
\]

rather than assuming:

\[
C=A+B
\]

unless numerical addition is actually intended.

## Predictions

A useful scientific extension of Causali E should produce predictions that can be compared with observations.

For example:

\[
P(C\mid A,B,E)
\]

may represent the probability of state \(C\) given causal/intervention state \(A\), retained conditions \(B\), and environment \(E\).

Backward inference could instead ask:

\[
P(A\mid C,B,E)
\]

This does not imply that physical time has been reversed.

It represents inference from an observed effect toward possible preceding causes.

---

## Time and Retrocausality

Claims involving physical retrocausality or time travel require separate evidence.

The mathematical ability to reconstruct earlier states from later measurements does not by itself demonstrate physical travel into the past.

Similarly, a branching mathematical model does not by itself prove the physical existence of branching timelines.

These concepts may remain within Causali E as hypotheses or exploratory models while being kept separate from established experimental results.
