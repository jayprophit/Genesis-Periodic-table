12. Metallic solid

At ordinary conditions lithium forms a very light metallic solid.

Its low density:

0.534\ {\rm g\,cm^{-3}}

makes it the least-dense elemental metal under ordinary conditions. 

That gives it a very high:

\boxed{\text{mass-efficiency score}}

for applications where mass matters.

But lithium metal is also highly chemically reactive.

14. Pressure dependence

Lithium is far more structurally complicated under pressure than its simple ambient structure suggests.

15. Electrochemical identity

The dominant chemical ion is:

Li^+

because lithium readily loses its 2s electron.

This makes lithium especially useful for ion transport.

The atom is small and the ion is light.

Therefore:

Li^+

can migrate through suitable liquids and solids.

That mobility is one of the central reasons lithium is technologically important.

30. Safety layer

Lithium demonstrates why safety must remain part of material identity.

* dendrite growth
* electrolyte decomposition
* thermal runaway

Those hazards belong to specific material/system states, not universally to every lithium compound.
