# MAT Safety and Dual Use

## Purpose

MAT is not a neutral repository for all possible knowledge without safeguards.

This document defines the basic principles for handling materials information that may present safety risks, environmental hazards, or dual-use concerns.

---

## 1. Scope

The safety and dual-use framework applies to information about:

- toxic chemical species;
- explosive or energetic materials;
- radioactive or radiotoxic materials;
- biological agents or toxins;
- hazardous process conditions;
- dangerous instrumentation or weapon-related research;
- process routes that could enable misuse.

---

## 2. Risk Classification

Researchers and curators must distinguish between:

- low-risk materials information;
- hazardous but lawful handling information;
- regulated or restricted information;
- dual-use process or composition information;
- information that should be treated as research-sensitive.

A safety classification should never be treated as a substitute for expert risk evaluation.

---

## 3. Safety Metadata

Record-level safety notes may include:

- hazard class;
- toxicity status;
- flammability;
- corrosivity;
- environmental risk;
- handling requirements;
- storage conditions;
- emergency response notes;
- exposure limits where relevant.

---

## 4. Dual-Use Considerations

Some materials information can be beneficial in one context and harmful in another.

Examples include:

- energetic formulations;
- high-risk synthesis chemistry;
- dangerous process parameters;
- radiation or biological hazard considerations;
- route-to-production knowledge for hazardous substances.

MAT should not suppress legitimate scientific knowledge, but it should make risk context explicit and traceable.

---

## 5. Responsible Handling

MAT records should:

- separate factual description from operational guidance;
- distinguish safe handling information from process optimisation details;
- avoid presenting dangerous information as routine or harmless;
- clearly mark hazardous conditions and uncertainty;
- keep provenance for any safety-relevant claim.

---

## 6. Public vs Restricted Information

MAT should avoid conflating open scientific information with restricted or dangerous operational details.

Where the project includes sensitive information, the associated record should clearly indicate:

- safety tier;
- review status;
- access level;
- justification for restriction or caution.

---

## 7. Human and Environmental Safety

Safety is not limited to immediate toxicity.

MAT should also track:

- environmental persistence;
- bioaccumulation risk;
- waste hazard;
- disposal methods;
- accidental exposure pathways;
- occupational risk.

---

## 8. Conclusion

The objective is not to suppress research but to ensure that materials information is recorded responsibly, with risk context, provenance, and appropriate handling discipline.

This is part of MAT's broader scientific integrity and governance framework.
