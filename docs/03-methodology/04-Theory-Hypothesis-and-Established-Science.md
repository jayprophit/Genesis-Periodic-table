# MAT Theory, Hypothesis and Established Science

## Purpose

MAT must separate these categories clearly:

- historical theory;
- exploratory model;
- testable hypothesis;
- established scientific relation;
- superseded or rejected idea.

This separation is essential to prevent the historical record from being mistaken for accepted science.

---

## 1. Historical Theory

Historical theory refers to earlier conceptual frameworks that were important in the development of a field, even if they are not currently accepted.

Historical theory may be preserved in MAT for:

- archival fidelity;
- lineage tracing;
- comparative analysis;
- understanding the evolution of scientific questions.

Historical status must remain visible.

---

## 2. Exploratory Model

An exploratory model is a structured conceptual representation that is useful for investigation, but not yet validated enough to be considered established science.

It may include:

- symbolic relationships;
- causal narratives;
- provisional equations;
- parameterisations still under test.

Exploratory models should not be recast as proven fact.

---

## 3. Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a statement that can be tested and falsified.

A valid hypothesis must specify:

- the variables;
- the conditions;
- the expected relation;
- the measurement method;
- the falsification rule;
- the evidence threshold.

If these cannot be specified, it is not yet a proper scientific hypothesis in MAT.

---

## 4. Established Science

Established science is a classification reserved for claims that have passed MAT's evidence, validation, and replication standards under stated conditions.

Established status requires more than a single article or a plausible model.

It requires a stable evidential record, clear conditions, and usually multiple independent confirmations or strong consensus under defined constraints.

---

## 5. Superseded or Rejected Ideas

Some ideas are historically valuable but later contradicted by stronger evidence.

MAT must not erase them.

Instead, a record should identify:

- prior claim;
- replacement claim or model;
- evidence that triggered revision;
- reason for supersession.

This preserves scientific lineage while keeping current records accurate.

---

## 6. Classification Logic

A useful practical logic is:

```text
Historical → Exploratory → Hypothesis → Validated → Established
```

with a separate branch for:

```text
Rejected / Superseded / Conflicting / Unresolved
```

A claim may remain in a provisional category for extended periods if evidence remains incomplete.

---

## 7. Conclusion

MAT does not confuse novelty with validation, and it does not confuse historical importance with scientific certainty.

The framework's strength comes from making those distinctions explicit within each record and across the repository.
