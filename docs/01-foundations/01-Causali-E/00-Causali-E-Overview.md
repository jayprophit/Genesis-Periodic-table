# Causali E

## Overview

Causali E is the author's developing conceptual framework concerning causality, constants, differences, potential states and transformations.

The original framework describes three principal conceptual roles:

\[
A = \text{difference or causal change}
\]

\[
B = \text{constant or retained condition}
\]

\[
C = \text{potential or resulting state}
\]

The original work explores combinations and transformations involving these variables and considers the possibility that different causal changes acting on retained conditions can create multiple future states.

The framework also explores ideas concerning:

- branching possibilities;
- forward inference;
- backward inference;
- changing and retained quantities;
- transformation;
- conservation;
- cycles;
- scale;
- motion;
- frequency;
- energy;
- time;
- nonlinear relationships.

## MAT Relationship

Causali E may be used as a conceptual method for asking questions of MAT.

For example:

Given an initial state \(S_0\),

a transformation \(A\),

retained constraints \(B\),

and environmental conditions \(E\),

what states \(S_1,S_2,\ldots,S_n\) are physically possible?

A more general mathematical form is:

\[
S_{t+1}=F(S_t,A,B,E,t)
\]

This is preferable to assuming that expressions such as:

\[
A+B=C
\]

must always represent ordinary arithmetic addition.

Within Causali E, operators may sometimes represent transformations or relationships rather than literal numerical arithmetic.

Any mathematical implementation must define those operators precisely.

---

## Research Principle

Causali E does not override experimental evidence.

Where the framework makes a prediction inconsistent with validated physical measurement, the prediction must be rejected or the model revised.

Where it predicts an unresolved relationship, that prediction may be recorded as a hypothesis for investigation.
