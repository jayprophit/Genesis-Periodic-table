# MAT Purpose and Scope

## Purpose
MAT exists to organise measurable and scientifically relevant information about matter within one extensible structure.

It has four principal purposes:

### Reference
Provide detailed records for elements, isotopes, compounds, materials and physical states.

### Relationship Mapping
Describe how records relate through bonding, transformation, processing, environment and application.

### Comparison
Allow properties to be compared using explicit units, conditions, uncertainty and evidence.

### Exploration
Provide structured data that can eventually support simulation, optimisation and materials discovery.

---

## Included Domains
MAT may include information from mathematics, physics, chemistry, materials science, nuclear science, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, optics, acoustics, crystallography, geology, biology, engineering, nanotechnology and environmental science.

Inclusion does not imply that every domain applies to every record.

---

## What MAT Is Not
MAT is not intended to redefine established measurements merely to fit a theory.

MAT is not a substitute for experimental verification.

MAT is not a claim that every hypothetical relationship is physically possible.

MAT is not intended to remove uncertainty from scientific information.

Instead, MAT records uncertainty and evidence status as part of the data.

---

## Scope Expansion
MAT may eventually extend from fundamental particles and atomic states through elements, isotopes, molecules, compounds, alloys, ceramics, polymers, semiconductors and nanomaterials while remaining compatible with the core MAT evidence and identification systems.
