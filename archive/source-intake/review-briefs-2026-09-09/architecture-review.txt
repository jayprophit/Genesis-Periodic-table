A few important things became clearer from the repository. MAT already has the correct **three-authority model**: authored Markdown for human-readable prose, canonical YAML/registries for scientific data, and generated publication/index assets for presentation. Your README also already defines conditions, units, measurement-vs-prediction status, provenance, uncertainty, time, scale, environment, manufacturing history, negative results, and separation of scientific evidence from speculation. That is substantially stronger than an ordinary ebook architecture. 

The `0000-Origin-State` structure also confirms the architecture we developed earlier: it isn't simply a chapter. It is a record package with `calculations`, `data`, `diagrams`, `experiments`, `graphs`, `images`, `models`, `relationships`, `sources`, and `tables`, plus its main manuscript and visual specification.  Hydrogen follows that expanded record-package model and already branches into isotopes, nuclear states, quantum/electronic states, ions, molecules, compounds, phases, processes, reactions, environments, applications, experiments, graphs, models and sources.  

I also confirmed the two things you were telling me about earlier: **both `downloadfiles/ebookcraft` and `downloadfiles/ebookcraft (1)` are actually in the repository**, along with the Walter Russell reference images and the MAT ebook production standard.  The newer `ebookcraft (1)` is itself a Vite-style application, so Codex can run it beside MAT and compare the experiences directly rather than guessing from screenshots. 

And MAT Studio is further along than a simple static reader. It already has a publication tree, periodic-table and model views, coverage filtering, bookmarks, local manuscript overlays, themes, font sizing, multilingual workflow UI, source-backed data views, evidence review, HTML/EPUB export, print/PDF preview and text-to-speech.  The root toolchain already contains separate validators for records, sources, relationships, assets, links, IDs, book synchronization and elements, plus publication builds and Playwright end-to-end testing. 

So the main problem is **not that MAT lacks engineering**. The larger gap is the layer between the engineering and the reader: information architecture, narrative editorial structure, professional-domain traversal, richer cross-reference semantics, true citation granularity, progressive depth and the visual/paginated feel of a major reference work.

The external benchmark supports that direction. EPUB 3.3 explicitly separates publication content, navigation, reading-system behavior and accessibility, rather than treating an ebook as one long HTML document.  Scientific references such as the NIST Chemistry WebBook organize one entity into selectable categories including thermochemistry, phase data, ion energetics, spectroscopy and literature references instead of forcing every dataset into the same view.  PubChem similarly separates human reports from programmatic REST access, which is close to the dual human/machine model MAT needs. 

The architectural change I would make is therefore evolutionary rather than destructive: **a MAT record remains the canonical scientific object, while its content is exposed through multiple domain lenses and multiple reading depths.** Hydrogen remains `MAT:0001`; chemistry, physics, medicine, engineering, history, education, AI and other views do not become competing copies of Hydrogen.
